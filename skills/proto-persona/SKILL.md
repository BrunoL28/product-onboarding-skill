## Purpose
Create an initial, assumption-based persona profile that synthesizes available user research, market data, and stakeholder knowledge into a working hypothesis about your target user. Use this to align teams early, guide initial design decisions, and identify gaps requiring validation.

This is not a validated persona—it's a "proto" (prototype) persona that evolves as you learn. A structured placeholder that prevents design-by-committee while acknowledging you don't have all the answers yet.

## Proto vs. Validated Persona
Proto: created in hours/days, based on assumptions + limited research, used to align teams early, evolves rapidly, good enough to start. Validated: weeks/months, extensive research, guides detailed design, stable, high confidence.

### Anti-Patterns
- Not validated research (it's a hypothesis).
- Not a replacement for user research (use it to guide research).
- Not demographic data alone (behavior explains use).
- Not permanent (evolve it).

## Application

### Step 1: Gather Available Context
User research, analytics, market data, stakeholder insights, product context. If missing context, don't fabricate—note gaps and plan research.

### Step 2: Define the Persona's Identity
**Name:** alliterative, memorable (e.g., "Manager Mike"). **Bio & Demographics:** behavioral, not just demographic; only what influences product decisions.

### Step 3: Capture Their Voice
**Quotes:** authentic and revealing (mindset, not facts). Use real quotes if available; else mark "[PLACEHOLDER—NEEDS RESEARCH]".

### Step 4: Document Their Context
**Pains:** specific, related to your product. **Trying to accomplish:** observable, outcome-focused. **Goals:** short- and long-term, personal and professional.

### Step 5: Understand Their Influences
Decision-making authority, decision influencers, beliefs & attitudes relevant to adoption.

### Step 6: Validate and Iterate
Share with team; tag "[ASSUMPTION—VALIDATE]" where uncertain; plan research; evolve or graduate to a validated persona.

## Common Pitfalls
1. Demographics without behavior → add behavioral context.
2. Treating proto-persona as fact → tag assumptions, plan interviews.
3. Creating 10 proto-personas → start with 1–2.
4. Fabricating quotes → use real quotes or placeholders.
5. Never validating → plan research sprints, evolve over time.

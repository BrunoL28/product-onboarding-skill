## Purpose
Conduct a systematic analysis of macro-environmental factors—Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Environmental, and Legal—that could impact your product or project. Use this to identify external opportunities and threats, inform strategic planning, assess market entry risks, and make data-driven decisions in the context of broader forces beyond your control.

This is not internal analysis—it's outward-facing assessment of the big-picture forces shaping your product's environment.

## The PESTEL Framework
1. **Political:** Government policies, stability, trade regulations, taxation
2. **Economic:** Growth rates, inflation, exchange rates, consumer spending
3. **Social:** Demographics, cultural trends, lifestyle changes, consumer attitudes
4. **Technological:** Advancements, R&D, automation, digital transformation
5. **Environmental:** Climate change, sustainability, resource scarcity, regulations
6. **Legal:** Compliance, IP rights, employment laws, health/safety regulations

### Anti-Patterns (What This Is NOT)
- Not competitive analysis (macro factors, not competitors).
- Not internal analysis (external environment).
- Not static (reassess regularly).

### When to Use This
Entering a new market, strategic planning, assessing viability in a changing environment, risk assessment, exec/investor pitches.

## Application

### Step 1: Define the Scope
Product name, analysis purpose, analyst, date, geographic scope, time horizon. Be specific and time-bound.

### Steps 2–7: Analyze each factor
For Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Environmental, Legal — examine the sub-factors, state the concrete impact on *your* product, keep it actionable and data-driven, cite sources. If a factor has low impact, say so honestly.

### Step 8: Synthesize Insights
Top Opportunities (with action), Top Threats (with mitigation), Strategic Recommendations.

### Step 9: Update Regularly
Annual review; update on trigger events; track changes over time.

## Common Pitfalls
1. Generic analysis → be specific and actionable.
2. Ignoring low-impact factors / forcing relevance → focus effort on high-impact areas.
3. No data sources → cite.
4. Analysis without action → synthesize into opportunities/threats/recommendations.
5. One-time exercise → review annually or on major external events.
